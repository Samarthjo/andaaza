About Andaaza

अंदाज़ा means a rough estimate: the number you arrive at when you can’t look it up but can reason your way close. Every day Andaaza asks three questions about India. You guess, see the real answer, and find out how many times off you were.

Why estimate?

The physicist Enrico Fermi was famous for questions like “how many piano tuners are there in Chicago?”, which sound impossible until you break them into smaller guesses you can make. Indian kitchens, shops and offices have always done the same thing as everyday hisaab. Estimating like this is how good planners, journalists and founders sanity-check numbers.

How scoring works

For each question we divide the bigger number by the smaller one, your guess or the answer. Guessing half or double both count as 2× off. Your day’s total is the geometric mean of the three questions, so one question being 10× off counts the same whether you guessed too high or too low. Any single guess counts as at most 1,000× off.

Where the answers come from

Census data, government ministries, the Election Commission, ISRO, official sports records and similar bodies. Each answer names its source and the year it refers to. Some figures, such as crowd counts, are estimates themselves, and the answer note says so. If you spot an error, tell us.
